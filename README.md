# TM Party Games

Live at **https://tm-party-games.web.app** (the old `before-and-after-6a096.web.app` address redirects there).

Players join with a 4-letter code (or QR) on their phone. A session has several games that share the same players. Every session starts on a game selection screen, where the host picks a game (host phone or main screen). The host phone can then switch straight to another game from the (foldable) game section at the top; "← Games" goes back to the selection screen. Players' phones always show the active game (or wait while one is picked):

- **Before & After**: each player records a **video**; its first and last frames become their **before** and **after**. The host (not a player) drives the main screen from their phone at `/host` using the same code: before → after → (pause to discuss) → video.

  **Where it comes from:** Before & After is based on [this video](https://youtu.be/S-iwuKrQP6A?t=1112&is=4r5NvLQ05E6I0vTx).

- **Photos**: each player submits one **photo**. The host shows them one at a time (Previous / Next) or everyone at once.
- **[BLANK] in a Box**: the host picks an object (e.g. a carrot, making it "Carrot in a Box"), two players and which of them may look inside their box. That player can peek on their phone as often as they like; the other chooses once to **swap** boxes or **keep** theirs. The stage announces the choice and swaps the boxes, then the host opens them one at a time. Which box holds the object is stored outside the session (`boxSecrets/{code}`), readable only by the host, the main screen and the peeker.

  **Adding objects:** drop a picture into `src/assets/box-objects/` and deploy. The file name is the object's name (`rubber-duck.jpg` → "Rubber duck in a Box"). A photo replaces a drawing with the same name, so `carrot.jpg` takes over from the placeholder `carrot.svg`.

  **Where it comes from:** [BLANK] in a Box is based on Carrot in a Box from *8 Out of 10 Cats Does Countdown* ([an example](https://youtu.be/FtaOYYrwCTo?is=P-b4SSDhu3FKgtxg)).

- **Noisemaster**: the host picks one player and a phrase of up to 8 words (sounds). That player's phone becomes a soundboard: "THE NOISEMASTER" over 16 blank buttons, 4 by 4. Each button plays a sound on the main screen. The phrase's words are on the board, and decoys fill the other buttons (the host picks them, or lets the rest be picked at random). The player has to press the buttons in the right order to make the stage say the phrase, which only the stage shows. A wrong press starts the phrase again. The clock starts at the first press and stops at the last word. The main screen plays and checks every press. Which sound is on which button is stored outside the session (`noisemasterSecrets/{code}`), so only the host and the main screen can read it. The host phone can open "The board" to see it.

  **Adding sounds:** like the music, sounds are never uploaded. Put one audio file per word in a `Noisemaster` subfolder of the main screen's music folder (`hello.mp3` → "hello"). The jukebox leaves that subfolder out. The board needs at least 16 sounds. After adding files, select the asset folder again on the main screen so it reads them.

  **Where it comes from:** Noisemaster is based on [this video](https://youtu.be/aKABUN2XHVo?is=bSptqy3YkNJX6ltL).

- **Flipchart**: each player draws a portrait on a 16-page flip chart on their phone, against the show's clock. The big screen puts the pages together in the show's pattern (pages 3, 9, 10 and 16 upside down).

  **Where it comes from:** Flipchart is based on [this video](https://youtu.be/huB67xaX47A?t=1039&is=VDg4xvuEvGGdvLPT).

- **Prize task**: each player brings a prize and submits one or more photos of it, any time from check-in. The host presents each prize in whatever order they like, picking which photo goes on the big screen. When the winner is declared at the end of the party, the big screen hangs the whole haul around the winner's headshot.

  Like Headshot, it isn't on the game picker: it's only added as a task from the scoreboard's "+ App game".

  **Where it comes from:** not one video: it's based on the prize tasks used on every episode of *Taskmaster*.

- **Headshot**: the headshots guests added at check-in, shown on the big screen one at a time or all at once, like Photos.

  **How it differs:** every other game has players doing something on their phone during it, but Headshot asks nothing of them: its photos are the ones already in the scoreboard's frames, checked in or not, in the scoreboard's order. Phones just say everyone's headshot is going up.

- **Secret Tasks**: the host types a pile of secret tasks (any number, one per line) in the **✉** tab in the host phone's header, which is there whichever game is on. Each task becomes a sealed envelope on a scattered pile on every player's phone, which shows nothing about what's inside. A player who checks in picks one envelope from the pile; the first to take an envelope has it, so nobody shares a task and nobody's ever changes (the main screen turns each pick into their dealt task, so it has to be open). Stragglers can be dealt a random one from the ✉ page. If there are fewer tasks than players, the rest wait until the host adds more; a removed player's task goes back on the pile. Tapping a task fixes its wording in place, and anyone already dealt it keeps it, corrected. Envelopes nobody picked roll forward to the host phone's next party (`hostLeftovers/{uid}`), offered as "Left over from your last party" until the host adds them or taps "Forget these". Once picked, a player sees their white envelope sealed with the TM wax seal: tapping it breaks the seal and the task is typed out in the typewriter font, and "Seal it again" closes it. Until then a **✉ Pick your secret envelope** button shows on their phone, and afterwards **✉ My secret task**; it appears at the top of their phone in every game, so they can pull it up at any time. The ✉ icon shows a burgundy dot while anyone hasn't opened their envelope yet (phones note the first opening at `secretTasks/{code}/seen`) or is waiting for one with the pile empty, and the ✉ page names them at the top. Its "Who has what" list shows each guest's state; holding a name shows that one task until you let go, and ✎ fixes it in place.

  **The reveal:** pick Secret Tasks as the game and tap **Start the reveal →** (anyone without a task sits it out). The big screen cycles through the players with a task in random order: "What was Sam's secret?" over a sealed envelope, while the room guesses. While it's sealed, the host can tap anyone still to come in the running order to bring them up instead. **Unseal Sam's envelope** opens it on the big screen and types out that one task (published to the session then, and not before); the same gold button then becomes **Next: Priya →**. **Scoring** is kept on the host phone: after unsealing, "Did Sam do it?" (Yes / No) and "Who guessed it?" (everyone else). Doing your task is a point, each correct guess is a point, and doing it with nobody guessing earns a bonus point. Points show as tally marks in the running order, and on the big screen only at the end. After the last player, the big screen shows "That's everyone!" with a scattered pile of every opened envelope and its task card, each with its player's tally marks; the host phone shows "Most points: …" and the final scores, where tapping a name fixes their turn. Until then the big screen never shows a task. Tasks are stored outside the session (`secretTasks/{code}`), so a guest can only read their own.

In Before & After and Photos a submission locks until the host unlocks that player. Each game lives in `src/games/<id>/`; `src/games/index.ts` lists what a game provides to the shared screens.

- `/` — main screen (shared display). `/play` — players' phones. `/host` — host remote. `/screen` — an extra, view-only copy of the main screen (e.g. in another room): same lobby, reveals and videos with sound, but no music (the songs stay on the main screen's PC) and no controls. After a refresh it asks for one click so video sound is allowed. `/light` — the light (below), on a computer with a webcam.
- Firebase: Anonymous Auth, Realtime Database (session state), Storage (media).

## The light
A running puzzle for the whole evening, after the Taskmaster task: a red light bulb goes on for a second, seemingly at random, and the players have to work out why. The secret: it goes on **2 seconds after anyone does the trigger** the host has picked: stops smiling (the original), starts smiling, raises their eyebrows, opens their mouth wide, raises an arm, lowers an arm, or touches their face. The arm and face-touch triggers need the camera to see people's arms and shoulders, not just faces. Faces are found across the room by first finding each person's body, then reading a close-up of their head (the face detector on its own only finds faces within a metre or two); the camera is asked for 1080p for that. The Camera panel's preview outlines each head: solid green where the face is read, dashed where only the head is found. In **Smiles** mode (picked on the host phone) `/light` shows two bulbs: green 2 seconds after someone starts smiling, red 2 seconds after someone stops. In **All at once** mode the camera watches for every trigger together, and `/light` shows a bulb per trigger, each in its own colour (in rainbow order: red: stops smiling, orange: raises eyebrows, yellow: touches their face, green: starts smiling, blue: raises an arm, violet: lowers an arm; green and red mean the same as in Smiles). Opening your mouth wide has no bulb of its own there: it sends the light spinning twice round all six bulbs in a rainbow (the Hue lamp follows the lit bulb's colour as fast as the bridge allows; the 0.5 s rule doesn't apply inside a spin), and the spin wins over plain flashes while it lasts, with no labels; the players have to work out which action lights which colour. Modes are listed in `MODES` in `src/light/triggers.ts`. In every mode each flash comes on 2 seconds after its action, for a second; when flashes overlap, the newest wins. The light (and the Hue lamp with it) never changes more often than every 0.5 seconds: a change that comes sooner waits for the rest of that half-second, so nothing is ever more than 0.5 s late, and one that's overtaken by a newer flash meanwhile is skipped.

- **Start it from the host phone (usually):** on 💡, tap **Start the light**. The big screen's computer runs it out of sight, with its webcam and Hue lamp: nothing shows on the TV, and since the main screen is always the window in front, the camera never slows down. The phone then walks through a checklist: which camera (with the heads it finds drawn on an empty frame; the picture never leaves that computer), and the Hue lamp (pick the lamps, **Flash the lamp** to test, or pair a new bridge from the phone). The camera and the bridge's key stay in the big screen's browser; the phone asks it for each change. The browser there asks once for the camera, and pairing a bridge may need its Local Network permission or certificate (below). After that it stays off until **Light on**, comes back by itself if the big screen reloads, and the 💡 icon pulses in the bulb's colour for each flash so the host knows it went. **Stop the light on the big screen** is in Party settings.
- **Or on another computer:** on a computer with a webcam that can see everyone, open `/light` (the main screen's lobby links to it) and enter the session code. The page shows just the bulb, since players may see it; its status and buttons (Hue lamp, Light it now, Pause, Stop the light) appear only while the mouse moves, and never show counts. Keep that window open and in front all evening: Chrome slows down background tabs and minimised or fully covered windows, and the light then misses things (the host phone warns when that happens). The page keeps the screen awake. One computer runs the light per session. If it finds nobody (common on Windows), move the mouse and click **Camera**: it shows a preview with how many people it finds (only while that panel is open), lets you pick the camera (until you do, it skips virtual cameras such as DroidCam, OBS or anything named "Virtual", and Windows Hello's infrared camera; the list is `NOT_A_WEBCAM` in `src/light/CameraSetup.tsx`), and can run the detection on the processor instead of the graphics card (some graphics drivers run it but find nothing). Those choices are kept in that browser.
- **Host phone:** the **💡 The light** section (on every view) is where the host picks the secret trigger, and shows how many people the camera sees and how many are "in" (e.g. smiling). It has **Light in 2 s** (for something the camera missed), **Light it now** (a test), **Pause camera** and **Reset the light computer** (so another computer can take over).
- **Privacy:** detection ([MediaPipe](https://ai.google.dev/edge/mediapipe/solutions/guide) face and pose landmarkers, models in `src/assets/light/`) runs in the light computer's browser; the picture is never shown, saved or uploaded. Only the counts go into the session. MediaPipe itself sends Google anonymous usage statistics (speed, frame counts, OS) about once a minute. Triggers are defined in `src/light/triggers.ts`; [docs/light-signals.md](docs/light-signals.md) lists everything the camera can detect, for picking new ones.
- **A real lamp:** Philips Hue lamps can flash with the light (in the bulb's colour on colour lamps; white lamps just go on), then go back to how they were: straight off if they were off, or on at the same brightness and colour. On `/light`, move the mouse and click **💡 Hue lamp**, find or type the Hue Bridge's address, press the bridge's round button and click **Connect**, then pick the lamps. The light computer talks straight to the bridge, so it must be on the same network. The bridge's key is kept only in that browser, never in the session. On a Mac, Chrome needs **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network**. If the bridge can't be reached, open `https://<bridge address>/api/config` once in that browser and accept its certificate (again after restarting Chrome, if needed). Reserve the bridge's address in your router so it doesn't change.

## Setup
1. Firebase console: create a project; enable **Authentication → Anonymous**, **Realtime Database**, **Storage**; add a Web app.
2. `cp .env.example .env` and fill it in.
3. `firebase use --add` (select the project), then `firebase deploy --only database,storage` to publish the rules.
4. `npm install && npm run dev`. Phones need to reach the dev server: set `VITE_PUBLIC_URL` to an https tunnel/LAN URL.
5. `npm run deploy` to ship to Hosting.

## Downloading everything
The main screen's **Download all photos & videos** button (hidden until the host taps "Show download button on the main screen" on their phone) zips every submitted file in the browser (one folder per player). Because it fetches the files with JavaScript, the Storage bucket needs a one-time CORS setting. In [Google Cloud Shell](https://console.cloud.google.com/?cloudshell=true) (project `before-and-after-6a096`):

```sh
echo '[{"origin":["https://tm-party-games.web.app","https://tm-party-games.firebaseapp.com","https://before-and-after-6a096.web.app","https://before-and-after-6a096.firebaseapp.com","http://localhost:5173"],"method":["GET"],"maxAgeSeconds":3600}]' > cors.json
gcloud storage buckets update gs://before-and-after-6a096.firebasestorage.app --cors-file=cors.json
```

(`cors.json` in this repo has the same content.) Players who were removed from the session are not included.

## Useless conversions
**📏** in the host phone's header turns a measurement into a real but useless unit, to read out when a task is judged: "39 dice tall is 65 LEGO bricks". Type the number, pick the unit (a task's own, like dice, or a common one: Height, Length, Weight, Time, Volume or Loudness) and tap **Surprise me**. It picks the unit closest to a round number, fractions included ("exactly 1/300 the height of Big Ben"), shuffling the closest few; **Surprise me again** goes down the list, and **Use a different unit** picks any. **Show on the big screen** puts the result on the TV over whatever is there until it's turned off or the host puts something else up. A task gets its own unit under ✎ → **📏 Measured in** (🎲 Dice fills in 16 mm). The units, about 100 each for height and length (mostly small things, so measurements land on whole numbers, plus famous people for height) and about 50 each for weight, time and volume (US cups and gallons, or ml and L), are in `src/conversions/units.ts`: rules and official figures where they exist, else generally accepted averages, which are always "about".

**Measured tasks:** ✎ on a live task has **Scoring**: By hand, Highest wins or Lowest wins. A measured task's points page has a box per contestant for their count or measurement, and the points follow by place: 5 for 1st, then 4, 3, 2, and 1 for everyone below, ties sharing a place (two 2nds, then 4th). Tapping a contestant's points sets them by hand, and that sticks. **DQ** (on every points page, in place of 0) scores 0 but keeps what they had, struck through; by default everyone below a DQ moves up, shown with a dashed "before" chip, and "Don't move them" turns that off for the task. Noisemaster on the run of show saves each finished go's time and restarts for the scoreboard by itself (fastest wins; ending a go early is a DQ). The logic is in `src/scoreboard/measured.ts`.

**Loudness:** ✎ → **📏 Measured in** → **🔊 dB meter** makes a task a sound task, read off a dedicated dB meter: type each reading as the meter shows it. An optional **🔇 DQ if louder than** makes any reading over it a DQ as soon as it's typed (exactly the line is safe; tap DQ to lift one). On a Lowest wins task, **LO** under the box is for a meter that can't read that low: it beats every reading, and LOs tie. 📏 compares a reading by counting sounds at once, since decibels don't divide ("94.2 dB is about 26 garbage disposals at once"); every sound is "about".

## Jukebox
Music is **never uploaded or committed**: the main-screen PC reads MP3s straight from a local folder. On the main screen (Chrome/Edge), click **🎵 Select asset folder** once and pick the folder (subfolders are included). The song list is shared with the host phone's **🎵 Jukebox** tab, which has Play/Pause, a Repeat toggle, volume, and tap-to-play. A song plays once (then stops) or repeats until you pause or pick another; it never advances by itself. Music pauses automatically while a video plays or the Noisemaster board is up.

**Task songs:** ✎ on any task on the run of show has "Song (optional)", picked from the jukebox's song list (the last one the main screen read, so it works with the PC off). The task's row and its page then carry a strip that plays that song once on the big screen, or pauses it. It's greyed out while the main screen's music folder isn't open, or if the song has left the folder. **Task transitions:** ✎ → **Transition** plays something on the big screen the moment you tap Start on a task, before you hand out the envelope: music, a picture, music and a picture, or a video on its own. Put the files in three subfolders of the music folder (like Noisemaster's, the jukebox leaves them out): `Transition music` (short tracks), `Transition pictures` (jpg, png, webp, gif, and an iPhone's heic, which is converted to a jpg in memory; one that can't be read is named on the curtain screen) and `Transition videos` (mp4, webm, mov). Select the asset folder again on the main screen after adding files; the "Raise the curtain" screen lists how many it found in each. Music plays once and stops (the jukebox stops too, and the task's song is greyed out until the transition is over); a picture comes down when the music ends; a picture on its own stays up until you put something else on the big screen; a video plays full screen with its own sound. The strip under "On the big screen" shows what's on, and one tap on it ends the transition. In ✎, "▶ Try it on the big screen" plays what's picked before you save, so you can check the track. The task's page keeps a strip to play it again, as often as you like. Once the curtain is up, the main screen reads the next two tasks' transition files into memory, a few seconds after the stage has loaded and one at a time, so a transition starts at once even from OneDrive (a video over 150 MB is read when it plays). It never reads the whole folder at once: a OneDrive file comes down in full the first time it's read, and that would fill the connection the stage's own pictures need. Start always works: a file that's missing is skipped, and the Start button says so beforehand.

Chrome remembers the folder but may ask for one click to re-grant access after a refresh ("Reconnect music folder"). Avoid picking `Downloads` or your whole home folder itself — Chrome refuses those; a dedicated folder like `C:\Music\BeforeAndAfter` works.
